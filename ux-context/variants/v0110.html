Report a car accident

Tell us what happened and we will open your claim. A claim is your request for us to pay for the damage, and this page is how it starts.

It takes about ten minutes. Nothing is timed, so you can put the phone down, deal with whoever needs you, and pick it back up. Nothing is sent to us until you press the Send report button at the bottom.

Answer what you can. If you do not know something, or the answer is in a document you cannot get to, leave it blank and keep going. We will fill in the gaps when we call you.

What this page asks for

  1. About you
  2. When and where it happened
  3. What happened
  4. Injuries
  5. Your vehicle
  6. The other driver
  7. Witnesses
  8. Photos

About you

So we know whose policy this is and how to reach you.

This is how we find your policy if you do not have your policy number with you.

We will use this number to reach you about the claim, so give us the phone you have on you today.

How would you rather we reach you?

The policy number is printed on your insurance card and is at the top of any email we have sent you. If your card is in the glovebox and you cannot get to the car, leave this blank. Your name and date of birth are enough.

Were you the one driving?

Leave this blank if you were driving.

When and where it happened

Close is good enough. Nobody expects you to have the exact minute.

Your best estimate is fine.

A street address, the two cross streets nearest to you, or a highway and the closest exit number. For example: Route 9 northbound, just past exit 12.

The two letter abbreviation.

Part 3 of 8

What happened

This is the part that matters most, and it is the part only you can give us.

Write it the way you would say it out loud. Which way you were going, what the other vehicle did, where each car ended up. There is no wrong way to write this, and you can add to it later if you remember more.

For example: raining and already dark. Or: dry, sunny, heavy traffic.

Did the police come to the scene?

The officer may have handed you a slip of paper or a card with this number on it. If you do not have it, leave it blank. We can request the report ourselves.

Part 4 of 8

Injuries

Tell us about anyone who was hurt, including yourself.

Was anyone hurt?

Say yes even if it seems small. Sore necks and backs often show up hours later, and it is easier for us if it is already in the report.

Include yourself, anyone riding with you, and anyone in the other vehicle if you know. A rough description is fine.

Did an ambulance take anyone to a hospital?

Part 5 of 8

Your vehicle

The car you were in, and what shape it is in now.

The brand of the car, which is usually the badge on the front.

The name of the particular car, which is usually written on the back.

If you cannot safely walk around the car to read it, leave this blank.

Where is the damage? (check every part that looks damaged)

Do not get out of the car to check if the road is not safe. Pick what you already know.

Did any airbags go off?
Can the car still be driven safely?

For example: still at the scene, the parking lot behind the gas station, my driveway, or the name of the tow yard it went to.

Part 6 of 8

The other driver

Every field here is optional. Fill in what you have. If the other driver has already left, or you cannot get to them safely, skip this part and keep going. We can track the rest down.

How many other vehicles were involved?

The company name on the card they showed you. Just the name is useful, even without the numbers.

Year, make, model, and color if you have them. If not, a blue four door sedan is a useful answer.

If more than one other vehicle was involved, tell us here what you know about the rest.

Part 7 of 8

Witnesses

Optional, but worth thirty seconds if someone is still standing there.

A name and a phone number for anyone who stopped, including a passenger in your own car. A person who saw it is the hardest thing to get back later, and the most useful thing you can write down right now.

Part 8 of 8

Photos

Photos help, but they are not needed to open your claim, and this is not the moment for them. Do not stand in traffic, and do not wait on a slow connection at the roadside.

We would rather send you a link and let you add them later, from somewhere safe and dry, on a connection that works.

The link goes to the mobile number you gave us in part 1. It works for thirty days and it does not ask you to create an account or remember a password.

Send your report

Nothing has been sent to us yet. If your connection drops before you finish, your answers stay on this phone until they can go through, and we will tell you plainly if something has not reached us.

When you press the button, we record the date and time. That is the official time of your report, and it can matter for your coverage, so it is worth sending now even if some answers are still blank.

You can correct or add to anything in this report after you send it. Most people remember more once they are calm, and that is normal. Call us at 1-800-555-0142 or use the link we text you.

Filing this report does not decide who was at fault. Fault is looked at later, once we have heard both sides and seen the damage. If you have questions about what this means for your policy, ask us on that call.

A person from Keystone will call you within one business day at the number you gave us in part 1.
