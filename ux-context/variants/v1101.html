Car crash report

Report a crash

You do not need your policy number, and you do not need an account. This takes about ten minutes, and you can stop partway and come back to it.

If anyone is hurt, call 911 first. Move somewhere safe if you can. This report can wait until you are ready.

What happens next

So you know what you are signing up for before you start.

  1. We got it.

    As soon as you send this, your report number appears on the screen and we text it to you.

  2. Someone calls you within one business day.

    The person handling your claim will call, usually sooner. You do not have to chase us.

  3. We look at the damage.

    They set up a time to see your car, or ask for a few more photos. You choose where it gets repaired.

  4. We tell you what you pay.

    If part of the repair bill is yours to pay, which is the part called your deductible, they give you the exact amount before any work starts.

Reporting a crash does not decide who was at fault. That comes later, and the person handling your claim will walk you through it.

1

Photos of the crash

Start here, while you are still at the scene. Photos tell us more than any answer below, so take them first and fill in the rest afterward.

  • Your whole car, from a few steps back

  • Each damaged area, up close

  • The other car and its license plate

  • The street, its signs and signals

You can add as many as you like, and you can add more later from the same link.

If you could not take photos, or it was not safe to, keep going. We can work without them.

3

When and where it happened

Close enough is fine. Nothing here has to be exact, and you can correct any of it later.

Your best guess is fine.

For example, the block you were on, or the intersection, or the highway and the nearest exit.

Two letters, such as OH.

Where exactly were you?
About how fast were you going? Optional

Nobody knows this exactly. A rough number helps, and leaving it blank costs you nothing.

4

Injuries, police, and towing

Answer what you know. If you are not sure, say so. That is a real answer here, not a gap in your report.

Was anyone hurt?

Anyone at all, including you, anyone riding with you, and the other driver.

Did police come to the scene?

If an officer handed you a card or a slip of paper, the number is usually on it. If not, skip this. We can ask them ourselves.

Can your car be driven right now?

A tow yard, a repair shop, your street, a parking lot. Whatever you know.

5

Your car

The car that was in the crash, even if it is not the only one on your policy.

Such as Honda or Ford.

Two letters.

Who was driving your car?
How bad is the damage to your car?

A rough sense is all we need. We are not asking you to guess at a repair cost, and this answer does not set what your claim is worth.

For example, the front bumper, the driver side door, the rear passenger side.

The other driver

The name on their insurance card, if they showed it to you.

Two letters.

Color, make and model, as much as you noticed.

7

What happened, in your own words

Last, and optional. If you would rather tell the person handling your claim when they call, leave this empty and send the report.

A sentence or two is plenty. Where you were headed, what you saw, what the other car did. Write it however it comes out.
