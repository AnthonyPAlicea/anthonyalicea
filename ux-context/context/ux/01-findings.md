# UX.md / Research findings

What research has established. Each finding is a constraint on what gets built, not a note in a report.

**F1. People do not have their policy number when they file.**
Ninety-one percent of first reports are filed away from home. The policy number is in a glovebox, an email, or nowhere. Asking for it first is the single largest cause of abandonment we have measured.
*Constraint: never require a policy number to begin. Identify people by something they carry in their head.*

**F2. Photographs come before words.**
People who add photos before writing anything finish the report far more often than people asked to describe the crash first. At the scene they can point a camera. They cannot compose a paragraph.
*Constraint: photos are the first substantive step. Written description is optional and comes last.*

**F3. The first question everyone asks is what happens next.**
Before anyone asks about money, they ask what is going to happen to them, their car, and their day. When the answer is not on the screen, they call. Roughly a third of calls to the claims line ask only this.
*Constraint: say what happens next, on the page, before asking for anything.*

**F4. Reports get abandoned and then restarted from zero.**
Progress is lost when a call comes in, a tow truck arrives, or a signal drops. People do not retype it. They give up and phone in.
*Constraint: save continuously. Make returning obvious and make the saved state visible.*

**F5. People are afraid of getting it wrong.**
They believe an error on the report will cost them the claim. They stall on questions they cannot answer exactly, especially time, speed, and damage estimates.
*Constraint: allow "I don't know" on any factual question. Say plainly that details can be corrected later.*
